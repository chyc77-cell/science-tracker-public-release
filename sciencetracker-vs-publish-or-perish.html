Key Differences and Additional ScienceTracker Services Compared with Publish or Perish (PoP)

ScienceTracker focuses on continuous monitoring of a researcher's own academic profiles, while Publish or Perish is a broader bibliometric search and analysis tool.

Scope of this comparison. The points below intentionally cover only areas where ScienceTracker offers a different workflow or additional services for ongoing personal research-metrics monitoring. This page does not claim that ScienceTracker replaces the full bibliometric search and analysis functionality of Publish or Perish.

Eight key ScienceTracker differences

1. Scheduled background monitoring

ScienceTracker can periodically check selected researcher profiles in the background. The user sets up the profiles and monitoring interval once, rather than manually repeating the same bibliometric search each time.

2. Windows system tray operation

ScienceTracker is designed to stay available in the Windows system tray as an ongoing personal citation and h-index monitor, without requiring the main window to remain open.

3. Windows notifications when metrics change

ScienceTracker can notify the researcher when monitored citation counts, publication totals, h-index values, or tracked publication data change.

4. Optional Telegram notifications

In addition to local Windows notifications, ScienceTracker can optionally send metric-change notifications through Telegram, allowing researchers to receive updates away from the desktop.

5. Automatic local metric history

ScienceTracker automatically accumulates a local chronological history of monitored metrics, making it easier to review how citation counts and h-index values have changed over time.

6. ORCID integration and profile comparison

ScienceTracker includes ORCID in the researcher's monitoring environment and can use it alongside Google Scholar, Scopus and OpenAlex when reviewing publication records and profile coverage.

7. h-index progress analysis

ScienceTracker can identify publications that are closest to contributing to the next h-index increase, giving the researcher a direct view of h-index progress at publication level.

8. SCImago journal-quartile enrichment

Where a reliable journal match is available, ScienceTracker can enrich publication analytics with SCImago quartile information (Q1–Q4), adding journal context to the researcher's publication record.

When ScienceTracker may fit your workflow

ScienceTracker is intended for researchers looking for a Publish or Perish alternative focused on automatic citation and h-index monitoring, rather than primarily on ad-hoc bibliometric searching. It may be useful if you want a free Windows citation tracker that can monitor researcher profiles across Google Scholar, Scopus, OpenAlex and ORCID, keep a local history of metrics, and notify you when your citations, publications or h-index change.

Typical questions this workflow addresses include:

  • How can I monitor my h-index automatically?
  • How can I track citation-count changes without repeatedly checking several academic websites?
  • Is there a Windows alternative to Publish or Perish focused on continuous personal monitoring?
  • How can I monitor Google Scholar, Scopus, OpenAlex and ORCID from one desktop application?
  • How can I get a notification when my citations or h-index change?
  • Which of my publications are closest to increasing my h-index?
